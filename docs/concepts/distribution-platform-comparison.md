# Distribution Platform Comparison: DistroKid vs TuneCore vs CD Baby

Date: 2026-05-05
Scope: Tyler James Drake release workflow (singles/EPs, independent artist ops)

## Side-by-Side Comparison

| Criteria | DistroKid | TuneCore | CD Baby |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pricing model | Subscription (annual), upload volume tied to plan | Subscription model with release tiers/plans | One-time per-release fee (single/album) |
| Upfront + recurring cost pattern | Low upfront for frequent releases; recurring annual fee to keep catalog active | Medium-to-higher recurring costs depending on plan + catalog size | Higher upfront per release; no annual catalog subscription required |
| Platform commission / distribution fees | Typically keeps distribution fee at 0% from net payout (store deductions still apply) | Plan-dependent, generally 0% distribution commission in current tiers | Takes a distribution commission on revenue (plus store deductions) |
| Payout cadence | Fast remittance once stores report and funds clear; artist can withdraw on demand | Regular remittance after store reporting and processing windows | Regular remittance after store reporting and CD Baby processing windows |
| Store reach | Major DSPs + broad optional add-ons | Major DSPs + broad global stores/social platforms | Major DSPs + broad store network |
| Rights/admin options | Optional paid extras (e.g., YouTube Content ID, sync/admin-related add-ons via partners) | Strong admin tooling and optional publishing/admin services | Publishing admin available (CD Baby Pro), sync/licensing support options |
| Lock-in / takedown constraints | Catalog availability depends on active subscription unless release-level legacy options are purchased; takedowns supported | Catalog tied to active plan/release status; takedowns and transfers supported | No annual lock-in for existing releases after fee is paid; takedowns/transfers supported |

Notes:
- All three platforms rely on downstream store reporting latency; none can pay before DSPs report.
- Terms and pricing change frequently; confirm on official pricing/terms pages before final commit.

## Recommendation for Tyler James Drake

Default platform: DistroKid.

Rationale for current catalog/release cadence:
- Best fit for frequent release cadence: recurring subscription is usually cheaper than repeated per-release fees.
- Faster operational loop for single drops: simple upload workflow and frequent payout access once funds clear.
- Good baseline reach for a solo independent catalog with optional add-ons only when needed.

When to choose differently:
- Choose CD Baby if release cadence slows and you want one-time payment per release without annual catalog subscription pressure.
- Choose TuneCore if you prioritize its specific admin/publishing feature set and the plan economics still beat DistroKid for your annual volume.

1. Confirm release volume target for next 12 months (singles, EPs, albums).
2. Build cost snapshot for your actual plan usage (base plan + required add-ons only).
3. Open/verify DistroKid account profile, tax, and payout routing.
4. Standardize metadata template: artist name, songwriter splits, ISRC/UPC policy, contributor roles.
5. Audit current catalog status by release and distributor to prevent duplicate live products.
6. Decide migration strategy per release: keep-as-is, takedown+reupload, or net-new only.
7. For first rollout, prepare one single with clean assets: master WAV, cover art, lyrics, credits, release date.
8. Configure rights options minimally at launch (only required add-ons).
9. Submit with at least 3-4 weeks lead time for playlist and content planning.
10. Verify post-launch checks: store links live, credits correct, splits/payees, claim status.
11. Document final platform decision and rationale in FR tracker for future repeats.
